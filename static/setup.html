C5 Print Hub setup

For the Flashforge Creator 5 Pro, unofficial. Nothing here leaves your own network unless you turn on the watcher with an online AI service.

1Printer

  1. On the printer screen, turn on LAN Only mode (network settings). The hub can only talk to the printer in LAN Only mode.
  2. The printer screen shows its check code in LAN Only mode, and its serial number on the device info screen. Type both below exactly as shown.

2Your PIN

Every phone asks for this PIN once. 4 to 8 digits.

3Cameras

The printer's own camera works by itself once the printer is connected.

Phone camera (optional)

An old phone above the bed gives the best view. Install an IP camera app on it (for example IP Webcam on Android), start its server, and type the address the app shows, like http://192.168.1.60:8080. The phone must be on the same Wi-Fi as this PC.

4Slicing settings from your Flash Studio

  1. Open Flash Studio on this PC with the Creator 5 Pro selected.
  2. Set up all 4 filaments with the presets you print with (your own tuned presets if you have them).
  3. Put any small model on the plate and click Slice plate.
  4. Come back here and tap Look for my latest slice.
The hub copies that file and slices every STL with exactly those settings. It checks every slice against it before anything is sent. Do this again whenever you change a preset in Flash Studio.

5Failure watcher (optional)

During a print, an AI looks at one camera frame every few minutes and pauses the print on a clear failure (spaghetti, a part knocked loose). It is off unless you set it up. A paid service bills you a small amount per check. The drying bed check uses it too; without it you check the bed by eye.
Make a key at console.anthropic.com. It is saved in config.json on this PC only.
Experimental - not tuned, may miss failures or pause good prints. The watcher's prompt and rules were tuned on Claude only.

6Open it on your phone

With the phone on the same Wi-Fi, open:
Windows asks once whether to allow Python on networks: choose Private networks only.
Keep it on your own network. Never forward this port on your router or put the hub on the open internet: anyone who reaches it can heat and move your printer. For outside access use Tailscale (see the README).
